The gap today

Onboarding stops at week one. Confirmation is left to memory.

Most HR platforms manage the first day well and go quiet after that. The probation period — where retention risk and legal exposure actually live — is tracked in spreadsheets, if it's tracked at all.

Week one

The curriculum ends with the induction.

Policy knowledge is delivered once, in a room or a PDF, and never tested. New hires learn the handbook, not the obligations that come with the role.

Months two and three

Reviews live in someone's calendar.

Probation check-ins depend on a manager remembering. There is no common record of what was covered, what was signed, or what still needs attention.

Day ninety

The confirmation decision has no trail.

When a confirmation is questioned, the evidence is scattered across email, paper, and memory — if it exists at all.

How it works

Four steps, from policy document to confirmed employee.

No new portal for HR to learn. CooptaIQ works from the documents your organisation already treats as authoritative.

  1. 01

    Upload

    Hand your handbook, code of conduct, and compliance manuals to CooptaIQ once.

  2. 02

    Generate

    The AI structures a role-specific curriculum and quiz bank from your own policy.

  3. 03

    Assess

    New hires train and sit a litmus-test assessment before probation formally begins.

  4. 04

    Confirm

    Probation milestones are tracked automatically, ending in confirmation and a payroll sync.

Legal defensibility

Not just onboarding software. A confirmation record.

Every module completed, every assessment score, every probation milestone — timestamped and exportable. If a confirmation decision is ever challenged, the record already exists.

  • Completed training log

    Per employee, per module, with timestamps.

  • Assessment scores on file

    The litmus test result behind every confirmation.

  • Probation review trail

    Manager sign-offs at each milestone, not just at the end.

  • Exportable on request

    For audits, disputes, or regulatory review.

The name

“Cooptatio was not paperwork. It was the formal act of admitting a new member — deliberate, witnessed, and recorded.”

In ancient Rome, cooptatio admitted a new member into the Senate or a collegium. CooptaIQ brings that same seriousness to the modern enterprise: the journey from new hire to confirmed employee, made formal, verifiable, and human.

The Roman Senate called it cooptatio. We call it CooptaIQ.
